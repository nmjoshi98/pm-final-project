What changed from Step 1 to V3?

V3: Connected the product to specific JTBD, especially helping busy adults remember to hydrate without adding effort.
Features were evaluated using effort vs. impact, making the output more useful for roadmap decisions.
Retention risks were explicitly identified, such as users forgetting to log, initial enthusiasm fading, and the product becoming another chore.
Monetization paths and tradeoffs were introduced rather than simply listing potential features.

Did the AI get smarter?

No. The PM input got smarter.

Adding JTBD, effort/impact, monetization, retention, and strategic tradeoffs forced the AI to move from “What could we build?” to “What should we build, for whom, and why?”

Key takeaway: AI accelerated the brainstorming and organization, but the PM frameworks provided the strategic judgment that made the output useful for discovery and prioritization.
- **Key takeaway: how does this prove the PM’s role is to think while the AI’s role is to execute?:** Key takeaway

The PM’s role is to think by defining the right user problem, applying frameworks like JTBD, assessing impact vs. effort, and making strategic tradeoffs. The AI’s role is to execute by rapidly generating, organizing, and refining ideas based on that direction.

AI provides speed and scale. The PM provides judgment and strategy.
