# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** Frontline Dispatcher / Logistics Coordinator. Coordinates daily deliveries, manages route changes, and monitors driver progress.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Keep deliveries moving with an accurate view of field activity, without chasing updates across multiple channels.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** Driver status updates lag 20–60 minutes, while reassignments take 8–15 minutes to reach drivers, forcing the coordinator to use calls, texts, and WhatsApp to establish what is happening and communicate current instructions.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** WhatsApp groups: Coordinate deliveries, share route changes, and get driver updates outside Velocity.
Text messaging (SMS): Send urgent instructions and confirm delivery status when app updates are delayed.
Phone calls: Contact drivers directly to verify their location, completed stops, and receipt of reassignments.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Check Velocity for delivery progress and identify statuses or assignments that may be outdated.
Call or message drivers through SMS or WhatsApp to confirm completed stops and current route progress.
Relay route changes directly rather than wait for reassignments to appear in the app.
Chase acknowledgment to confirm drivers received the changes and know which instructions to follow.
Reconcile driver responses with Velocity as delayed updates arrive, checking for conflicting information.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** Core frustration: The coordinator needs to act on a route change but cannot trust the dashboard or confirm that the driver received the reassignment. They must leave Velocity, call or message the driver, and chase acknowledgment before they can confidently move forward.

The broken moment: The system meant to coordinate deliveries becomes another source of uncertainty the coordinator must manually resolve.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** Evidence from the supplied research summary: Dispatch reassignments take 8–15 minutes to reach drivers with no push notification, while driver status updates lag 20–60 minutes, leaving completed stops marked “in progress.”

Supporting behavior: Dispatch teams use calls, texts, and WhatsApp to coordinate outside Velocity because platform updates do not reliably reflect field activity.

This supports the core frustration: coordinators must manually establish what is happening rather than trust the dashboard. This is summarized evidence, not a verbatim interview quote.
